# Concept first: every image has a thought behind it

No image in a RHAD post is decoration. Each one is a **visible version of what the headline says**. The reader should be able to read the headline, look at the picture, and feel they are the same idea told twice: once in words, once in objects.

Never generate, pick or place an image until the concept brief below is written. If you cannot fill a line, you do not understand the post yet. Ask or go back to the brief.

## The concept brief (write this before any prompt)

Save it at the top of `image-prompts.md`.

```
CONCEPT BRIEF
Message: <the one thing the viewer should understand, in one plain sentence>
Headline it serves: <the exact headline, or "none, the image carries it">
Visible metaphor: <one real, physical scene a person could photograph>
Element map:
  - <object> = <what it stands for in the message>
  - <object> = <what it stands for>
  - red thread / red cube = <what it connects, what it resolves>
Why this and not a cliché: <one sentence: why this scene is specific to this message>
Tension and resolution: <what looks unresolved on one side, what resolves it on the other>
Read-test: <what a stranger would say the image is about with the headline covered>
Left out on purpose: <objects that would confuse or repeat the headline>
Source or reference: <Media Read finding, or the reference images attached>
```

### Rules

1. **One message, one metaphor.** Two ideas means two posts or two slides.
2. **Every object earns its place.** If an object has no row in the Element map, remove it from the prompt.
3. **Words and picture divide the work.** The headline states the claim. The picture shows the situation behind it. They do not repeat each other. A label inside the picture names only what the eye cannot identify alone.
4. **Specific over generic.** An hourglass and two in-trays say "enquiry waiting for an answer". A glowing brain says nothing. No stock robots, orbs, handshakes, rocket ships, lightbulbs, puzzle pieces or arrows hitting targets.
5. **Real objects from the audience's day.** Receipts, trays, notebooks, envelopes, tool boxes, microphones, ring binders, typewriters. A Singapore SME owner should recognise the object from their own counter or desk.
6. **Tension, then resolution.** Most strong images have a gap, a pile, a wait or a mess on one side and a clean connection on the other. The red thread or the red cube is the resolution.
7. **Red means connection and next step.** It is the one colour in the scene. It joins things. It is never decoration.
8. **Read-test it.** Cover the headline. Could a stranger guess the topic from the image alone? If not, change the objects, not the headline.
9. **Use the Media Read.** If the user supplied footage or photos, the metaphor starts from what is really in them. Do not invent a scene that contradicts real media.
10. **No claims in pictures.** No fake dashboards, ratings, results, testimonials or customers. A picture may show a situation. It may not prove a result.

### The pattern behind all of them

- **Two-part readable headline**: the problem or situation in black, the turn in red.
- **One real object scene** that stages the situation, built from a small number of recognisable objects.
- **A red resolution**: thread, bridge, ribbon or arrow, ending at the red cube. The cube is the RHAD45 signature and the point where loose ends join.
- **Paper-and-halftone ground**: torn paper layers, halftone dot patches, tactile texture, so the whole thing feels handmade and honest rather than digital.
- **Labels as torn strips** only where an object needs naming.

## Applying this to other posts

**RHAD agency posts** use the same method with the RHAD look (monochrome portraits, tile-and-thread collage, red thread and cube). The metaphor is chosen from the client's world or the lesson, not from a library of stock ideas. Example: a post about overseas expansion should stage a real shipment, a passport desk or a map table, not a globe icon.

**Carousels**: each slide's image carries its own single idea and its own element map. Do not reuse one metaphor across slides unless the carousel is about one object.

**Supplied media**: write the Media Read first. The concept brief then says how the real footage serves the message, and any generated companion image extends it.

## Quick self-check before generating

- [ ] I can say the message in one sentence.
- [ ] Each object in the prompt has a row in the Element map.
- [ ] A stranger could guess the topic from the image alone.
- [ ] The image shows the situation. The headline states the claim. They do not repeat each other.
- [ ] No object is a cliché from the banned list.
- [ ] Nothing in the picture claims a result.
